App Store screenshots are PNG or JPEG, with no transparency, 1 to 10 per device size, and the pixel size has to match one of Apple's listed sizes exactly. For iPhone, Apple's largest display class (iPhone with Dynamic Island, large display) takes **1260×2736, 1290×2796 or 1320×2868 portrait**; 1290×2796 is the most common because most templates and tools (ShotStudio included) export it.

Apple updated its screenshot specification in October 2026: the page now drops the inch labels, adds a "Required device sizes" section, and lists the new iPhone Duo. This post walks through the current page, with every size for every Apple device below, and the failure modes we see most.

## Which iPhone sizes are required

Apple's page (as of 2026-11-11) says three things that decide what you upload:

1. Under "Required device sizes": *"iPhone: At least one screenshot for iPhone with Dynamic Island (medium display)."* That is the 1179×2556 / 1206×2622 class (iPhone 17, 17 Pro, 16 Pro and similar), called 6.3" in older guides.
2. Under "Display scaling": *"If your app's user interface is consistent across multiple device sizes and localizations, you only need to provide screenshots for the highest required resolution. App Store Connect automatically scales them down for smaller device sizes."*
3. Each smaller class carries a fallback note. For the medium Dynamic Island display: *"If screenshots with the accepted sizes aren't provided, scaled screenshots for iPhone with Face ID (large display) are used."* And the Face ID large display row is still *"Required if app runs on iPhone and screenshots for iPhone with Dynamic Island (large display) aren't provided."*

The required line and the fallback notes pull in different directions, so before you submit, open your version page in App Store Connect and check which iPhone slots it marks as needing screenshots. If you want zero ambiguity, upload a set for the medium Dynamic Island display as well as your large-display set.

## The failure modes I see most often

These are the reasons indie iOS apps get rejected or rendered weirdly on the carousel:

- **A size from the wrong row.** 1284×2778 is a valid Face ID large-display (6.5") size, but it is not a Dynamic Island large-display (6.9") size. Drop it into the wrong slot in App Store Connect and the upload fails. Match the file to the display slot you're filling.
- **PNG with transparency baked in.** Even one alpha-channel pixel kills the upload. Flatten to a solid background before exporting.
- **Status bar drawn on top.** Apple strips and re-renders the status bar. If you draw one, it'll get stripped and the bottom of your image will end up taller than expected. Don't include it.
- **Round corners drawn on the screenshot.** Same story — the App Store applies its own corner radius. If you draw them, you get a double-radius effect.
- **JPEG compression artifacts in solid-color regions.** App Store carousel re-compresses on the fly; starting from JPEG instead of PNG compounds the artifacts. Submit PNG when you can.
- **One color space across the set, then a P3 outlier.** Apple won't reject this but the carousel will render the P3 image with subtly different saturation. Stay in sRGB across the whole set.

## What you actually need to ship

One to ten screenshots per locale. Most indies ship three. The order on the App Store carousel matters more than people realize — your hero shot has to do all the work in the first 200ms of someone scrolling, because shots 4 onward are behind a "show more" tap that almost nobody takes.

The safe default for what each shot should *do* is: **shot 1 sells the outcome, shot 2 shows the workflow, shot 3 shows the differentiator**. What that means in practice changes per vertical — compare [fitness app screenshots](/screenshots-for/fitness-apps), where shot 1 has to imply momentum, with [finance app screenshots](/screenshots-for/finance-apps), where shot 1 is one number large enough to read across the room, or [VPN app screenshots](/screenshots-for/vpn-apps), where shot 1 has to prove the connection is safe before anyone reads a word.
